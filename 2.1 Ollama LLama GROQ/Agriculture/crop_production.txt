CROP PRODUCTION AND MANAGEMENT MANUAL 

1. INTRODUCTION TO CROP HUSBANDRY AND SEASONALITY
Crop production is the cornerstone of agricultural systems, categorized primarily by seasonal climatic requirements:
- Kharif Crops (Monsoon Season: June to October): Require high water volumes and warm temperatures. Examples include Oryza sativa (paddy rice), Zea mays (maize), Glycine max (soybean), and Gossypium spp. (cotton).
- Rabi Crops (Winter Season: November to April): Require cooler climates and moderate watering. Prime examples include Triticum aestivum (wheat), Cicer arietinum (chickpea), and Brassica juncea (mustard).
- Zaid Crops (Summer Season: March to June): Short-duration crops grown between Rabi and Kharif, typically including Cucurbitaceae family crops (cucumbers, melons) and fodder crops requiring irrigation.

2. SEED SELECTION, SEED TREATMENT, AND SOWING PROTOCOLS
The foundation of a high-yield harvest begins with quality planting material:
- Seed Certification & Vigor: Farmers must procure Certified or Foundation seeds with a minimum genetic purity of 98% and physical purity of 97%. Seed viability should be verified via standard germination tests (target >85% germination rate).
- Seed Treatment (Bio-priming & Chemical Prophylaxis): To protect emerging seedlings from seed-borne and soil-borne pathogens, seeds are treated sequentially. Fungicidal treatment using Carbendazim or Thiram at 2.0 to 2.5 grams per kilogram of seeds is mandatory. For legumes, Rhizobium inoculants are applied to boost biological nitrogen fixation.
- Sowing Geometry & Depth: 
  * Wheat: Sown using seed drills at a depth of 4–5 cm, keeping a row-to-row spacing of 20–22 cm to optimize canopy closure and light penetration. Seed rate is maintained at 100 kg/ha.
  * Rice: Traditional transplanting involves raising wet nursery beds, uprooting 21-to-25-day-old seedlings, and planting 2–3 seedlings per hill at a shallow depth of 2–3 cm under puddled conditions.

3. PHENOLOGICAL STAGES AND CRITICAL GROWTH WINDOWS
Crop management requires synchronized interventions aligned with vegetative and reproductive phases:
- Vegetative Phase: Focuses on root establishment and tillering/branching. Nitrogen top-dressing is commonly deployed here.
- Flowering and Anthesis Phase: The most sensitive window across almost all cereal and pulse crops. Absolute moisture stress or high heat shock during flowering directly impacts pollen viability, resulting in barren spikelets or flower drop.
- Grain/Fruit Filling Phase: Translocation of photosynthates from source leaves to sink organs (grains/pods). Premature senescence caused by drought or pests severely shrivels grain quality.

4. HARVESTING, THRESHING, AND POST-HARVEST LOSS MITIGATION
- Physiological Maturity Determination: Harvesting too early results in shriveled, immature grains with low test weight; harvesting too late leads to shattering losses, lodging, and pest attacks. Moisture content must be monitored closely.
- Drying Thresholds: Grains must be sun-dried on clean tarmac sheets or passed through mechanical continuous-flow dryers until safe storage moisture thresholds are achieved—specifically, 12% for cereals (wheat, rice, maize) and 8-9% for oilseeds.
- Storage Management: Cleaned grains should be stored in hermetically sealed silos, jute bags treated with approved safe insecticides, or Purdue Improved Cowpea Storage (PICS) bags to prevent infestation from storage pests like Tribolium castaneum (rust-red flour beetle) and Sitophilus oryzae (rice weevil).
