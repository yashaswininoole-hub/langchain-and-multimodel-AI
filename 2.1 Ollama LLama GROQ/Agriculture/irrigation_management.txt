IRRIGATION AND WATER MANAGEMENT REFERENCE MANUAL

1. CROP WATER DYNAMICS AND SCHEDULING CRITERIA
Efficient water management depends on matching irrigation events with real-time evapotranspiration (ETc) rates and physiological sensitivities:
- Critical Irrigation Stages: Certain phases cannot sustain water deficit without permanent yield loss. For instance, Wheat requires absolute irrigation at Crown Root Initiation (CRI, 21 days post-sowing), tillering, flowering, and grain-filling stages. Rice requires continuous submergence or saturated soil during vegetative growth to suppress weeds and maintain redox potential.
- Soil Moisture Tension: Utilizing tensiometers or capacitance probes to trigger irrigation when soil matric potential drops below prescribed thresholds (e.g., -30 to -50 kPa for vegetable crops).

2. ADVANCED MICRO-IRRIGATION INFRASTRUCTURE
Moving away from inefficient flood/furrow irrigation toward precision delivery mechanisms:
- Drip (Trickle) Irrigation: Network of polyethylene mainlines, sub-mains, and lateral tubes fitted with pressure-compensating emitters delivering water drop-by-drop precisely to the rhizosphere. Achieves up to 90–95% Water Use Efficiency (WUE), eliminates deep percolation losses, prevents weed proliferation between rows, and enables precise fertigation (injecting soluble fertilizers directly through irrigation lines).
- Sprinkler Irrigation Systems: Pressurized overhead sprinklers mimicking natural rainfall patterns. Highly suitable for undulating topography, sandy soils with high infiltration rates, and closely spaced field crops like groundnut, pulses, and fodder.

3. RAINWATER HARVESTING AND WATERSHED CONSERVATION
- On-Farm Water Storage: Excavation of farm ponds (lined with low-density polyethylene sheets to prevent seepage) to capture high-intensity monsoon surface runoff for life-saving supplemental irrigation during dry spells.
- Groundwater Recharge Structures: Construction of check dams, contour trenches, percolation tanks, and recharge shafts to reverse falling water tables and maintain sustainable aquifer yields across semi-arid farming districts.
